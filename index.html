01 / THOUGHTS SAMPLE DRAFT

Does every idea
need an architecture?

Finding the line between useful preparation and postponing the first step.

Read the draft ↗

Before choosing a framework or drawing a system diagram, write down the problem, who faces it, and what a useful outcome would look like.

A small prototype can help distinguish decisions that matter now from decisions that can wait. The question is not whether architecture matters, but which constraints are already real.

What needs to last, and what should remain easy to change?

02 / EXPERIMENTS SAMPLE IDEA

Measure first.
Then optimize.

One question, two implementations, and a closer look at the conditions of a benchmark.

Explore the idea ↗

The proposed experiment: choose one operation and compare two implementations using the same inputs.

Look at execution time and allocations across several input sizes. Record the environment and test setup alongside the numbers; a result without context is difficult to use.

This is an experiment outline. No measurements or performance improvements are claimed.

03 / SIDE PROJECTS SAMPLE CONCEPT

A small corner
of the internet.

A personal website with room for professional work and ideas still taking shape.

Open the note ↗

A portfolio and a journal answer different questions. One helps someone understand the work and the decisions behind it. The other makes room for curiosity and exploration.

A shared visual language connects them. A project can begin as a notebook entry and eventually become a case study, with links between the process and the finished account.

04 / GUIDES SAMPLE DRAFT

Write down
the decision.

A short structure for keeping the reasoning behind a technical choice.

Read the guide ↗

Start with context: what problem are you solving, and which constraints matter?

List the options you considered, then explain the choice and its trade-offs. Include what would make you revisit it.

Keep the record short enough to maintain. A useful decision note helps the next person understand the reasoning, not just the outcome.

05 / THOUGHTS SAMPLE DRAFT

Where could AI
actually help?

Start with a recurring problem in the team’s work, then explore the tools.

Read the draft ↗

A useful starting point is a small, recurring task with a clear definition of a good result. It gives an experiment something concrete to evaluate.

Consider the review effort as well as the time saved. A faster first draft is only useful if the whole task becomes easier to complete reliably.

This is a question for exploration, not a claim about an AI rollout or its results.
